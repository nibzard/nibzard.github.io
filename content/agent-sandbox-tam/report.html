The bull case for agent sandboxes: $26.1B by 2030

Every AI agent needs a private computer to run code, use a browser, and learn. We estimate that vendors who rent these computers bill about $1.54B a year at the end of 2026. In the bull case, that grows to $26.1B in 2030. The mid case gives $13.3B. If prices fall 20% a year and big buyers move 30% of the work in-house, bull volumes give only $10.3B. The bull case is a bet on volume and on vendors keeping their price.

The thesis

A sandbox is an isolated computer where an agent works. Agents are moving from chat to tasks, and every task needs a safe place to run. So sandbox time is a usage meter for the agent economy. It grows with the number of agents, the work that each agent does, and the time that each task runs.

Vendor revenue is what outside vendors, clouds, and labs bill for this time. Total demand also counts work on buyers' own servers. Model tokens are out of scope.

Five reasons for the bull case

  1. The revenue is real and compounding. Modal reports a $300M run rate for sandboxes alone1. E2B passed $100M, from $8M a year earlier2. Daytona projected $100M in October, from $1M four quarters earlier3.
  2. Sandboxes become a default part of every agent. Among US enterprise AI builders, code sandbox use rose from 11% to 40% in one year4. Bull setting: 2030 adoption is 1.5× the mid case. For example, 75% of software organizations instead of 50%.
  3. Each agent does more work. Agents now run for hours, split work across parallel sub-agents, and retry until tests pass. Labs also train agents with reinforcement learning (RL) in thousands of parallel environments. At one of the three leaders, this work brings about 70% of revenue7. Bull setting: 25% more tasks for each adopter in 2030, for 1147 billion sandbox runs a year.
  4. Model labs send work to vendors. Anthropic lists 11 sandbox providers for its Managed Agents5. OpenAI lists nine partners for its Agents API6. Companies that build their own agents also rent: Ramp runs its coding agent on Modal9. Bull setting: vendors keep their 2026 share of organization work (60%) instead of falling to 55%.
  5. Personal agents create a mass market. Meta Muse passed 10 million setups about six weeks after launch8. OpenAI, Google, xAI, and Anthropic all ship similar agents, each with its own cloud computer. Bull setting: 280 million people use one in 2030 (10% of weekly AI users), and vendors host 25% of them.

Where the extra revenue comes from

The model counts work: organizations × adoption × tasks × share that needs a sandbox × runs × resources × list price. Vendor revenue is the vendor-hosted part, at 65% of list price, plus platform fees. Prices stay flat in the bull case.

How firm the numbers are

  • 2026 is an estimate, not a measurement. Three vendors report about $500M of run rate. If they hold 30–60% of the market, the market is $833M–$1.67B. The model's $1.54B sits inside that range, but no source measures their share.
  • 2027 carries the most risk. The market must almost triple in 2027 (+191%). The leaders grew 12–37× in 2026, so this is a sharp slowdown, but still a large step. Growth then falls to +67% in 2030.
  • The risks are linked to the upside. More volume invites competition and in-house builds. Alone, 30% in-house gives $18.6B and prices down 10% a year give $19.2B. With 30% in-house and prices down 20% a year, bull volumes give $10.3B, below the mid case. At 30% a year, they give $7.89B.

What history says

  • The forecast needs company-like growth for a whole market. Cloud infrastructure and RPA grew 20–27% a year. The mid case needs 71%, faster than Snowflake or Shopify grew as single companies. The bull case needs 103%. Only demand shocks, Zoom and CoreWeave, grew faster, and only for two years.
  • The bull case for 2030 is about the size of AWS in 2018 ($25.7B13). AWS sold many services; sandboxes are one workload.
  • Adoption is not the same as vendor revenue. Containers and serverless became standard, but clouds took much of the revenue. RPA was called inevitable and reached $3.6B in 202414. CoreWeave grew 22× in two years and still lost $1.17B in 202513.
  • Slower growth still gives a real market. From $1.54B, 30% a year gives $4.41B in 2030 and 50% a year gives $7.81B.

Who captures the value

More sandbox use does not guarantee vendor revenue. The same work can run in four places:

  • Independent vendors. The model's provider list has more than 60 products, from sandbox platforms to browser and RL environments. Vendors win on fast starts, saved state, parallel copies, and Windows, macOS, and GPU machines. Some buyers replace their own sandbox platforms with a vendor once volume grows12.
  • Clouds. All large clouds now sell sandboxes. They count as vendor revenue here, but they set the price floor.
  • Lab runtimes. Anthropic includes sandbox time at $0.08 an hour10. The model's mid list price for the same machine is $0.131 an hour. Labs are both a channel and a competitor5, 6.
  • In-house. OpenAI bought Ona to run Codex agents inside customer clouds11. The largest buyers can build their own fleets.

The model measures revenue, not gross margin. Vendors that rent servers from clouds partly resell compute. Snowflake shows that a large business can still grow on rented cloud servers. Their margin depends on how full they keep their machines and on features that clouds lack. Past markets point to four outcomes: a new default layer; a specialist market that grows 30–50% a year; cheap, bundled execution, with value moving to security, saved state, and control; or a market held by clouds and labs. The outcomes differ in who keeps the revenue.

Bottom line

The bull case asks a whole market to grow like a breakout company. That is demanding. The firmer claim is this: agents create a new class of compute demand, and even the growth rates of past markets give a market of several billion dollars by 2030. The winners will turn sandbox-hours into security, control, and lasting developer habits.

What to watch by the end of 2027

  • The three leaders together pass about $1.57B of run rate (35% of a $4.49B market).
  • More than half of enterprise AI builders use code sandboxes.
  • The price of one sandbox-hour stays near today's level, and lab runtimes keep outside vendors as options.
  • Leaders disclose gross margins that stay high as they grow.
