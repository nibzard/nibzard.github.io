Agent sandboxes: the case for a $13.3B market by 2030

AI agents need a private computer to run code, use a browser, and practice tasks. Selling that computer time is a new market. In our central case, vendors bill $1.54B a year at the end of 2026 and $13.3B in 2030. Even a harsh bear case leaves $7.04B in 2030.

What we measure

A sandbox is an isolated computer where an agent runs code, operates a browser, or trains. It can be thrown away after one task or kept for weeks.

  • Vendor revenue is what outside vendors bill for this computer time, after discounts, plus platform fees. Model labs that sell sandbox time count as vendors.
  • Total demand values all sandbox work at list prices, including work that buyers run on their own servers.
  • Out of scope: model tokens, ordinary model training and inference, generic web hosting, and orchestration-only software.

The case in five points

  1. The revenue already exists, and it grows fast. Modal reports a $300M run rate for sandboxes alone1. E2B passed $100M2. Daytona projects $100M, up from $1M four quarters earlier3. These three vendors grew 12–37× in 2026.
  2. Agent products now include a sandbox by default. Among US enterprise AI builders, code sandbox use rose from 11% to 40% in one year4. Anthropic and OpenAI both sell sandbox time with their agent services. Both also connect to E2B, Modal, Daytona, and others5, 6.
  3. Volume compounds. More organizations adopt agents, each runs more tasks, and each task needs retries and parallel attempts. The mid case grows from 50.4 billion sandbox runs a year in 2026 to 614 billion in 2030. Prices hold up: E2B earns about $0.03 per sandbox start. The model bills about $0.04 per vendor-hosted run, including persistent computers and storage.
  4. Training agents is a second engine. Labs train agents with reinforcement learning (RL) in thousands of parallel environments. About 70% of Daytona's revenue comes from this work7. RL and evaluation reach $2.62B of vendor revenue in 2030.
  5. Personal agents add a new population. Meta Muse, OpenAI Dots, and Google Gemini Task give each user a cloud computer. Muse passed 10 million setups about six weeks after launch8. Most of this runs on the providers' own servers, but it still adds $1.11B of vendor revenue by 2030.

How the number is built

The model counts work, not companies. Each of five segments follows the same chain, with low, mid, and high values for every input:

total demand = organizations × adoption × tasks per adopter × share that needs a sandbox × runs per task × resources per run × list price vendor revenue = total demand × share bought from vendors × price paid vs list price + platform fees

In the mid case, vendors host 56% of the work in 2026 and 43% in 2030. Buyers pay 65% of list price on average. Personal agents count people instead of organizations.

Cross-checks

  • Reported revenue. Three vendors report about $500M of run rate. If they hold 30–60% of the market, the market runs at $833M–$1.67B a year. The mid case says $1.54B. The low case is set so that it covers the three reported run rates.
  • Read 2026 as a year-end run rate. These vendors grew so fast that their calendar-year revenue is only about 30–50% of their year-end pace.
  • Hardware spend. Futurum expects $23.7B of spend on server CPUs that run sandboxes, orchestration, and agent tools in 2026, and $165B in 20309. Our total demand is 15% and 25% of that. That is a plausible share.
  • No sandbox-only forecast exists. Published forecasts cover all AI agents (about $50B in 2030)10. They are too broad to test this model.

What could make it smaller

  • Big buyers build in-house. OpenAI bought Ona for Codex sandboxes11. Meta owns Manus, a former E2B customer. Cognition and Replit built their own infrastructure. If buyers move 30% of vendor-hosted work in-house by 2030, vendor revenue falls to $9.53B.
  • Prices fall. Free clones of E2B's interface exist. Anthropic includes sandbox time at $0.08 an hour5, below the model's $0.13 list price. A 10% price cut each year gives $9.77B in 2030.
  • Both together give $7.04B. That is still 4.6× the 2026 level. The risk is lower margins and fewer winners, not missing demand.
  • Revenue is concentrated. A few labs drive most RL demand, and they are the most likely to build their own.

The scenarios are bounds, not a confidence interval. The high case sets every input to its maximum at once. Its 2030 total demand is 3.9× the whole server CPU market, so do not use it as a forecast.

Conclusion

The evidence supports a vendor market of about $1.54B a year now and $7.04B–$13.3B in 2030. Demand is the safer part of the case. The open question is who captures it: independent vendors, cloud providers, model labs, or the buyers themselves.

What to watch:

  • Run rates from the three leaders and from cloud providers in 2027.
  • The share of lab agent sessions that run in outside sandboxes.
  • The price of one sandbox-hour.
  • Whether labs rent or build the environments for RL training.
