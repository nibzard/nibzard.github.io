The bull case for agent sandboxes: $26.1B by 2030

Every AI agent needs a private computer to run code, use a browser, and learn. Vendors that rent these computers bill about $1.54B a year today. In the bull case, that grows to $26.1B in 2030. For this, growth must slow only from 12–37× this year to about 2× a year.

The thesis

A sandbox is an isolated computer where an agent works. Agents are moving from chat to tasks, and every task needs a safe place to run. So sandbox time is a usage meter for the agent economy. It grows with the number of agents, the work that each agent does, and the time that each task runs.

Vendor revenue is what outside vendors bill for this time, including model labs that resell it. Total demand also counts work that buyers run on their own servers. Model tokens and ordinary hosting are out of scope.

Five reasons for the bull case

  1. The revenue is real and compounding. Modal reports a $300M run rate for sandboxes alone1. E2B passed $100M, from $8M a year earlier2. Daytona projects $100M, from $1M four quarters earlier3. The bull case needs only about 2× a year from here.
  2. Sandboxes become a default part of every agent. Among US enterprise AI builders, code sandbox use rose from 11% to 40% in one year4. Bull setting: 2030 adoption is 1.5× the mid case. For example, 75% of software organizations instead of 50%, and 27% of enterprises for workflows instead of 18%.
  3. Each agent does more work. Agents now run for hours, split work across parallel sub-agents, and retry until tests pass. Labs also train agents with reinforcement learning (RL) in thousands of parallel environments. About 70% of Daytona's revenue comes from this work7. Bull setting: 25% more tasks for each adopter in 2030, for 1147 billion sandbox runs a year.
  4. Model labs send work to vendors. Anthropic lists 11 sandbox providers for its Managed Agents5. OpenAI lists nine partners for its Agents API6. Companies that build their own agents also rent: Ramp runs each session of its coding agent in a Modal sandbox10. Bull setting: vendors keep their 2026 share of organization work (60%) instead of falling to 55%.
  5. Personal agents create a mass market. Meta Muse passed 10 million setups about six weeks after launch8. OpenAI, Google, xAI, and Anthropic all ship similar agents, each with its own cloud computer. Bull setting: 280 million people use one in 2030 (10% of weekly AI users), and vendors host 25% of them.

Where the extra revenue comes from

The model counts work, not companies: organizations × adoption × tasks × share that needs a sandbox × runs × resources × list price. Vendor revenue is the part that vendors host, at 65% of list price, plus platform fees. The bull case changes only the five 2030 inputs above. Prices stay flat.

Why the bull case is credible

  • It starts from observed revenue. The 2026 figure, $1.54B, is inside the range that reported run rates imply. Three vendors report $500M. If they hold 30–60% of the market, the market is $833M–$1.67B.
  • It assumes slower growth than today. The bull case grows about 2× a year. The three leaders grew 12–37× in 2026.
  • It fits within hardware spend. Futurum expects $165B of spend in 2030 on server CPUs that run sandboxes, orchestration, and agent tools9. Bull-case total demand is 46% of that. In 2026 it is 15%.
  • It survives the main risks. Big buyers can move 30% of the work in-house, and prices can fall 10% a year. With both, the bull case still gives $13.7B in 2030, about the mid case (Figure 3).

What the bears say, and the answer

  • “Big buyers will build their own.” Some will: OpenAI bought Ona12, and Meta owns Manus. But the labs' own agent services list outside vendors as options5, 6, and the long tail of agent builders rents.
  • “Sandboxes are a commodity.” Basic isolation is cheap: Anthropic includes sandbox time at $0.08 an hour11. Value moves to saved state, persistence, scale, and enterprise controls, and vendors now sell all of these. Lower prices also increase volume.
  • “The clouds will win.” That changes who wins, not the size of the market. Cloud sandbox revenue counts as vendor revenue here.

What to watch

The bull case is on track if these things happen by the end of 2027:

  • The three leaders together pass about $1.57B of run rate (35% of a $4.49B market).
  • More than half of enterprise AI builders use code sandboxes.
  • Lab agent services keep outside sandbox vendors as default options.
  • The price of one sandbox-hour stays near today's level.
