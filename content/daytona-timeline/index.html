daytona.io publishing timeline

Items per month across four content types, Jun 2023 – Oct 2026 · 202 blog posts (articles + guides) · 34 product-update posts · 164 changelog entries (44 recovered from Wayback) · 89 glossary definition pages · 8 launch weeks · domain rank from DataForSEO

Analysis. The blog is the through-line: 2–15 posts every month since Jun 2023, with July spikes (15 in 2023, 13 in 2024, 12 in 2026) and two zero-months around the 2025–26 new year. Everything else is an era. The glossary was a one-time push — all 89 pages went up between Feb 2024 and Feb 2025†, and not one was added after. Update posts peaked in Oct 2024 (9), then slowed to a trickle: 7 posts in the 17 months after Dec 2024. The changelog has two lives: a 2024 open-source run of 44 releases in 9 months (v0.1.0–v0.44.0, lighter shade — later deleted from the site and recovered from the Wayback Machine), then silence, then a restart at v0.101.0 (Sep 10 2025) that has run at ~9 entries per month since. The vertical line (10 May 2025) splits it cleanly: before it, 71 blog posts and 46 glossary pages per year; after it, blog output fell ~38% to 44 per year while the changelog became the publishing engine at 81 entries per year — release notes replaced editorial content. Launch weeks tell the same story in events: five ran before the line (monthly through fall 2024, then LW5 ending eight days before Niko left), three after (Jun, Aug, Oct 2025) — and none in 2026.
Rank (top panel). Domain rank (DataForSEO, 0-100) is the outcome measure. It climbed 4 to 46 across the tenure line, held 46-49 through late 2025, then jumped +12 in Jan-Feb 2026 to 61 and has sat there since. That jump was not content: those months carry 0 and 1 posts, and the new links point at the homepage, from funding trackers and regional press covering a raise, plus the early-2026 wave of AI-tool directories. The late-2025 climb tracks the changelog restart cadence from Sep 2025. Rank pulled 2026-10-30; monthly snapshots, none yet for Oct 2026.
